# Rules

The rules every scene of the video keeps. `DESIGN.md` describes the design they produce, and `SCENES.md` applies it scene by scene.

## Scope and structure

- The full album song, 4:16; no short version. Three acts by world line.
- One scene per lyric line, five to seven seconds; the instrumental parts in scenes of four to five bars. Every cut lands on the beat grid.
- Scenes are episodes of the show, and the show's world is fair game: the meter, the D-mail, the time machine, SERN, the IBN 5100, the dates.
- The first and last frames are the same.

## People

- No person is ever drawn: no figures, faces, bodies or silhouettes.
- Only Kurisu is present as a person at all, and only as a light. Everyone else is a light, a point, a charge, a line.

## Text

- No text explains the picture. Text on screen is of four kinds only: titles, clocks and dates that count, axis labels and scales, and numbers that belong to an object in the picture. No labels naming things, status lines, readouts of what the picture already shows, lists, stamps or checklists.
- The lyric is exact: every line shown, readable, each character no earlier than it is sung, with its romaji word by word. The lyric is small and in a fixed place per act; full-frame type only in the hook. Title-safe area, 96 px.
- No stills of the video inside scenes, except the opening's glimpses and the outro's rewind.

## Look

- One style throughout: threads and points of light. A scene in a visibly different manner from its neighbours does not fit.
- No photorealism. When something looks wrong, more realism is not the fix.
- Nothing toy-like: no thick shaded solids, no fat rope, no chunky links, no flying drops.
- Abstract over literal: blood, wounds, bodies and bonds as abstractions.
- Not too dark, and not empty: a dim scene with little in it is a failure.
- Modern, not retro; none of the usual clichés; decoration comes from the scene's idea; nothing copied from the show or from another video.

## Motion

- It moves. A long scene develops; it does not hold one idea.
- Smoothly: nothing jerks on the beat. The music is felt through what happens, not through jolts to the frame.
- One continuous move per scene; no cuts inside it.
- One dominant direction per scene; things separated in depth, never crossing at one point.
- Things do not vanish unexplained; an ending is shown.

## Accuracy

- Readings and dates follow the show's chronology. Where a scene shows real science, it is accurate.

## Working method

- Stills before any clip; a clip before a full render; the full video first at review quality. Sheets, clips and review renders use the same renderer as the final.
- Renders run on the discrete GPU.
